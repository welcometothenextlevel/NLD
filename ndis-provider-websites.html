/ The problem

Most NDIS websites are written for the NDIA, not for people.

Open a dozen provider sites and you'll find the same thing: walls of registration-group numbers, acronyms with no explanation, and a stock photo of two people laughing at a laptop. Technically compliant. Completely unhelpful to the person actually reading it.

The people on your site are a participant working out whether you offer what they need, a family member checking whether you seem trustworthy, and a support coordinator with eleven other providers open in other tabs. All three want the same three things quickly: what you do, where you do it, and how to reach a human.

Get that right and the enquiries follow. It isn't a design problem so much as a plain-writing problem — and it's the part most providers never get help with.

/ What the site has to do

What an NDIS website actually has to do.

Four things, in this order. Everything else is decoration.

Say what you offer in plain words

Your services described the way a participant would describe them, not the way they appear in a price guide. Registration groups can sit further down the page for the coordinators who want them.

Be genuinely readable

Real contrast, generous type sizes, proper heading structure so screen readers work, and text that reflows when someone zooms to 200%. For this sector that isn't a nice-to-have.

Name your service areas

"Melbourne's northern and western suburbs" beats "servicing Victoria" every time — both for the person reading and for the local searches you want to appear in.

Make contact unmissable

A phone number that dials on a tap, a short form that doesn't demand a plan number, and WhatsApp if that's how your participants prefer to talk. One clear path, repeated on every page.

/ Straight answers

NDIS questions.

We've built for a registered provider and we know what the pages need to cover — services by registration group, service areas, referral pathways and clear contact routes. What we don't do is give you compliance advice; you know your obligations far better than we do. We make sure the information you give us is presented clearly and accessibly.

That's built in rather than bolted on: proper heading structure, colour contrast that passes WCAG AA, text that reflows when zoomed, alt text on images and keyboard-navigable menus. For NDIS providers it matters more than for anyone else, and it's also just better for everybody.
